01 — The Foundation

A complete land, sea & air rescue system.

The Kuya Lifesaver Foundation delivers a three-tier emergency response system built on internationally proven frameworks.

On the ground and water, USAR-certified local teams operate a dedicated sea ambulance and pre-staged response vehicles on both sides of the Caticlan strait — modelled on the INSARAG-aligned Urban Search and Rescue standard used by professional disaster response agencies worldwide. Certified mass casualty, structural collapse, typhoon, and earthquake extraction capability for island communities that currently have none.

In the air, a dedicated medevac helicopter retainer covers cases beyond boat range across Boracay, Siargao, and El Nido — STEMI, major trauma, anything requiring Iloilo or Cebu — in under 25 minutes.

The Model
Modelled directly on Australia's Westpac Lifesaver Helicopter service — which has saved over 10,000 lives since 1973.

Together, these three layers constitute the first complete land, sea, and air emergency medical system ever deployed across the Philippines' most visited and most isolated island destinations. Funded entirely by Filipino ticket buyers. Built by Filipinos. For Filipinos.

The Ati Tribe

The Foundation also stands with the Ati Tribe — the indigenous community of Boracay, the original landowners of the island, forgotten in the rush of its expansion. Funds for legal battles over their titled land. Housing. A community centre. Lasting infrastructure for the people who were here first.

What every ticket also builds.

Beyond emergency response and immediate charitable funding, the Foundation delivers a full programme of community infrastructure — built using founder-supervised EPS panel and shotcrete construction, at a fraction of the cost and timeline of conventional government delivery.

School Classrooms

EPS panel classrooms built to DepEd standard across underserved communities. Resourced with tablets and teacher training. ₱800K–₱1M per classroom versus ₱2.5M through DPWH conventional construction. Four to six months from foundation pour to handover — versus four to five years through government channels.

Flood Levee Walls

Critical flood levee walls in at-risk communities nominated by ticket holders and prioritised by the Foundation board. Wet and dry shotcrete applied to EPS panel levee structure. Permanent, typhoon-rated. ₱2M–₱5M per zone.

Disaster Response

When typhoons hit, when the ground shakes, when the floods come — our USAR-trained Filipino responders are already on the ground. Not waiting on a government convoy from Manila. Not waiting on outside help. Search. Rescue. Triage. Stabilisation. Done by Filipinos, for Filipinos, in the first hours that matter most.

Disaster Relief Domes

Airform monolithic dome shelters deployed post-typhoon and post-earthquake. Operational within weeks of a disaster event. FEMA near-absolute protection rating. Permanent community structures for remote barangays. ₱3M per community dome.

Community Health Clinics

EPS panel health clinics constructed in geographically isolated and underserved barangays (GIDA). Addresses the chronic absence of primary healthcare access in island and rural communities. Foundation-funded, founder-built, locally staffed.

National Expansion — Year 3+

Open-application model for underserved communities nationwide. GIDA-eligible barangays apply through the Foundation for construction programme support. The Boracay launch chapter becomes the template, replicated across every region that needs it.

Every ticket funds a draw. Every draw funds a cause. Kuya Giveaways is the engine — and these children are the reason.
03 — The Future

A hand on every teacher's shoulder.

The Philippines has one of the most dedicated teaching workforces in the world. Men and women who show up every day for their students — often in classrooms that are overcrowded, under-resourced, and stretched far beyond what any single teacher should carry alone.

The shortfalls are not for lack of heart. They are the weight of a rapidly growing nation working hard to catch up.

Every teacher standing in front of forty children with no printed materials, no reliable power, and no preparation time deserves better. Every child sitting on a plastic chair hoping to learn deserves better.

The AI Education & Curriculum Programme

An offline, Filipino-language, MATATAG-aligned AI teacher's assistant. Built to run on a cheap tablet, through any brownout, in any classroom — no internet required.

It drafts lesson plans. Generates worksheets. Grades papers. Fills subject-expertise gaps for teachers currently doing the work of three. It is not a replacement for the teacher. It is a hand on their shoulder.

Developed quietly through Years 1–3. Proven in real classrooms with real teachers and real children. Then released to the nation as fully open-source in Year 4 — free to every school in the Philippines, forever.

The Foundation builds it. Proves it. Gives it away. Because that is what this foundation is for.

04 — Why Now

Because waiting isn't an option.

Somewhere in the Philippines tonight, a child will go to bed hungry. A grandmother will have a stroke on an island with no ambulance. A village will go through another typhoon in homes that can't take another one. None of that is new. None of it is a secret.

What's new is this — for the first time, there is a legal, proper way to run a charity raffle in the Philippines without it being shady or illegal. The door has finally opened. So we walked through it. We registered a foundation. We built the systems. We got the permits. We did it the right way so that every peso a Filipino spends on a ticket can be tracked, accounted for, and seen with their own eyes going where it was meant to go.

The dream of winning a bike, a car, a life-changing prize — that's the spark. But the reason we built this isn't the prizes. It's the rescue boat. The clinic. The classroom. The flood wall. The kids who deserve someone to actually show up for them.

We're not waiting for someone else to fix this. Filipinos buying tickets. Filipinos winning prizes. Filipinos building the country. That's the whole idea.
